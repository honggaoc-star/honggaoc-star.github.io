About Honggao Cao

Independent research and practice on understanding, evidence, prediction, AI systems, institutions, uncertainty, and human experience.

Present research

Honggao Cao's current work includes AI-specific research on evaluation, model error, evidence sufficiency, human–AI performance, provenance, risk, value, and management. Separate lines of inquiry examine how questions remain unresolved, how information becomes understanding, how prediction and attribution interact in adaptive settings, and how institutions, opportunities, relationships, and luck shape outcomes.

Essays and broader questions

Alongside the formal research, independent essays examine luck, causal attribution, social systems, outcome pursuit, life stages, choices, and the relationship between achievement and lived experience.

Research approach

The work begins with concrete judgment problems: what is being evaluated, what evidence bears on it, which assumptions are doing work, how outcomes should be interpreted, and what remains uncertain. Analytical frameworks, literature review, essays, and interactive tools are used according to the question. AI may be the subject being studied or a working tool used in research, writing, software development, or asset creation; that tool use does not turn independent work into one AI theory or validate its claims.

Intellectual continuity

Earlier research and analytical work on quantitative finance and risk, survey methodology and data quality, economics and social science, and program evaluation provide relevant experience with measurement, uncertainty, incentives, and institutional decision-making. That continuity informs the current agenda without treating every earlier publication as part of a predetermined AI research program.

Professional and academic background

Honggao Cao's current independent research is informed by a Ph.D. from the University of Chicago and earlier research at the University of Michigan's Survey Research Center on survey measurement, data quality, household economics, and retirement research. His professional experience in quantitative analytics, model validation, model risk management, and quantitative finance adds practice with decisions where evidence, assumptions, controls, and institutional responsibilities all matter.
