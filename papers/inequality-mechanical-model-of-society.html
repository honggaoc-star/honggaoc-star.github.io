Inequality in the Context of a Mechanical Model of Society

Nature, Nurture, and the Separation of Mechanism from Correction

Honggao Cao · October 2026

How can we tell what an effort to reduce inequality has actually achieved?

What this paper helps us understand

A change in inequality gives us an outcome to explain, but it does not tell us what an intervention accomplished. This paper offers two comparisons: what would have happened without the correction, and what further improvement was possible. Its mechanical example helps readers keep those questions separate; applying the comparisons to a real society requires evidence about the process, available interventions, and their timing.

Abstract

Inequality can rise even when efforts to moderate it are making a substantial difference. This paper examines how accumulation and correction interact through a mechanical baseline in which heterogeneous endowments and accumulated outcomes reinforce one another. Starting from equal outcomes, the baseline produces a rising mean and increasing inequality. A budget-balanced transfer example shows why equalizing current stocks need not prevent renewed divergence: inequality subsequently rises, but remains substantially below the uncorrected outcome. We distinguish that corrective effect from the further improvement attainable under the available interventions. The latter comparison requires an efficient frontier defined for a specified mechanism, correction technology, timing, and horizon. Together, the baseline and frontier organize questions about what correction changes, what continues to generate inequality, and what further combinations of mean and inequality are achievable. The purpose is to make correction intelligible without losing sight of the continuing process it modifies.
