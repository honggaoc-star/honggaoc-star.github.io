The World Might Not Be as Complex as We Thought

The Roles of Scale, Perspective, and Information in Human Understanding

Honggao Cao · September 2026

When something seems complex, what would help us understand it better?

What this paper helps us understand

Collecting more observations does not always make a confusing situation easier to understand. We may need a different viewpoint, a way to connect records we already have, or an explanation of how accounts at different scales relate. This paper helps readers identify which of these tasks an inquiry faces. The distinction matters because each calls for different work, and a clearer explanation need not remove the practical difficulty being explained.

Abstract

A phenomenon can appear fragmented or difficult to understand because the conditions under which we encounter it reveal only part of its structure, or because we have yet to connect the information already available. We examine this possibility through a framework that distinguishes two analytical stages: a world, object, or process becomes available as information under a particular scale and perspective, and an analyst organizes that information into understanding. This distinction helps locate different sources of apparent complexity. A relevant relationship may remain inaccessible under the operative observation conditions; its informational basis may be available but insufficiently organized; or descriptions obtained under different conditions may each be intelligible while their connection remains unclear. Physical, organizational, temporal, and mathematical examples develop these possibilities and show why additional observations and better understanding need not advance together. An aggregation identity provides an explicit example of a bridge between descriptions at different scales. We then situate the argument in earlier work on observation, scale, information, explanation, and connections among theories. The resulting account offers a way to investigate where an apparent difficulty arises and what kind of inquiry may address it. The world can remain rich and genuinely difficult while some of its apparent complexity reflects how its relationships become accessible and connected to us.
