Why Important Questions Are Often Difficult

Significance, Persistence, and the Composition of Unresolved Inquiry

Honggao Cao · September 2026

Why do significant and difficult questions become prominent among unresolved inquiries?

Abstract

Why do questions that matter so much often remain difficult to answer? This paper distinguishes obstacles to resolving a particular question from the processes that shape which questions remain under active inquiry. Questions may stay open because evidence, methods, or other necessary conditions are missing. Across a population, difficulty can slow resolution while significance can sustain pursuit. As more tractable questions are answered and less-valued inquiries are dropped, the remaining active questions can become richer in both significance and difficulty. A competing-risks model shows that this enrichment does not necessarily create statistical dependence: with independent attributes at entry and separable survival weights, the two properties remain independent among survivors. Examples from disease research, mathematics, markets, and artificial intelligence distinguish persistent obstacles from changing questions. The practical task is to identify why a particular inquiry remains open before deciding what further progress requires.
