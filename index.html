Independent research and practice

Questions about understanding, evidence, systems, institutions, and human experience.

Honggao Cao pursues independent research and builds practical tools. AI is both a subject of study—through evaluation, risk, management, and human–AI performance—and a working tool used in pursuing other questions about information, prediction, society, luck, outcomes, and life as it is lived. Research Questions

Several independent lines of inquiry provide starting points. Related questions can inform one another without becoming a single theory or transferring evidence between projects.

Understanding and inquiry

Why do some questions remain difficult, and how does information become understanding?

Work in this area examines unresolved inquiry, apparent complexity, perspective, scale, and the relational connections needed to turn revealed information into understanding.

Start with difficult questions · Explore apparent complexity

Prediction and change

What happens when prediction, action, and explanation alter the system being studied?

Research considers adaptive settings in which prediction and attribution become linked, and development frontiers change as some problems are resolved while others become more prominent.

Read about prediction and attribution · Follow the advancing frontier

Society and human experience

How do capabilities, opportunities, institutions, relationships, and luck shape outcomes over time?

Independent research and essays examine how present positions emerge, how outcomes alter later conditions, and how success, progress, and life experience should be interpreted.

Read the synthesis manuscript · Visit Research on Luck

AI evaluation and model error

How should an AI response be judged before anyone claims to know why it failed?

Authority-first evaluation separates discrepancy classification from localization, causal attribution, and consequence assessment; related work treats proposed hallucination mechanisms as hypotheses requiring evidence.

Read the evaluation framework · Examine plausible mechanisms

AI value, risk, and management

How can value and risk support decisions that remain revisable as evidence changes?

Research asks how an evolving value case and an independently assessed risk case can inform continuing decisions to proceed, adapt, expand, restrict, or stop AI-enabled activity.

Human–AI performance and provenance

What does knowing that AI was used add to an evaluation?

Work on provenance asks when disclosure is useful evidence and when direct measures of quality, contribution, retained human authority, verification, or institutional purpose are more informative.

Related Paths

These are navigational connections between independent works—not claims of a unified theory, shared validation, or automatic transfer of conclusions.

Observed error and possible cause

Move from evaluating a delivered AI response to investigating candidate mechanisms.

The authority-first analytical framework classifies discrepancies before causal attribution; the mechanisms manuscript organizes hypotheses about possible causes. Neither treats an observed error as proof of its mechanism.

Evidence, value, risk, and management

Move from evaluating a defined AI use to a bounded, revisable management decision.

Evidence-Centered AI Evaluation asks what evidence supports reliance; Return-Weighted Risk keeps value and risk cases distinct; PAIM v0.1 provides a separate bounded application for organizing continuing management. PAIM's release validation does not validate either publication or establish real-world organizational effectiveness.

Luck, institutions, and outcomes

Move among distinct views of how outcomes arise and change over time.

Luck in a Puzzling World, How We Get Where We Are, and Society as a System in Pursuit of Outcomes offer related but independent paths through attribution, institutions, conditions, expectations, and progress.

Completed manuscript v1.1

How We Get Where We Are

People, Institutions, and Luck in an Unequal World

Honggao Cao · September 2026 · 54 pages

Why can relatively modest differences in individual capabilities coexist with much larger differences in rewards, resources, and influence?

This manuscript follows how personal abilities, opportunities, resources, and relationships become consequential through institutions, and how outcomes change the conditions of later activity. Research and examples from housing, farming, health care, technology, finance, and irrigation connect present positions with the histories that produced them. The discussion also asks what counts as improvement, for whom, through which means, and over what period.
